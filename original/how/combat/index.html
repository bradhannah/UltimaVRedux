Combat, by the numbers

Every swing is one roll against a target number set by two attributes. Damage is a second roll, armour a third. Here is the whole calculation, one fight worked through, and what monsters leave behind.

Will it hit?

The game sets a target number, then rolls from 1 to 30. Meet or beat the target and you hit.

  1. Pick the attacking attribute. Dexterity, unless the weapon bludgeons (Club, Mace, Two-Handed Hammer, Spiked Helm, Spiked Shield); then Strength. Monsters that bludgeon use their Strength too.
  2. Work out the target. 30, plus the defender's Dexterity, minus the attacking attribute, halved (drop any fraction).
  3. Roll. A roll at or above the target hits. A target of 1 or less always hits.

Armour does not help here

Armour never makes you harder to hit. It only takes damage away once a blow lands. Level and equipment add nothing to the attribute either.

  • The die is lopsided. The game halves a roll from 0 to 60 and counts 0 as 1, so a 1 comes up twice as often as most numbers and a 30 half as often.
  • A sleeping target counts as Dexterity 1. An attacker with DEX 20 then hits 84% of the time.
  • The Glass Sword and the Jeweled Sword skip the roll and always hit. The Glass Sword kills almost anything in one blow, then shatters. The Jeweled Sword does no damage at all.
  • A creature's Dexterity is rolled again for each fight: from 4 below its Bestiary figure to 3 above.
  • Turns are not taken in rounds. Each fighter waits 36 minus its Dexterity ticks between turns, so DEX 30 acts every 6 ticks and DEX 15 every 21.
  • A miss is not wasted. It lands on a random square next to the target and can hit whoever stands there, your own party included.

How much damage?

  1. Weapon roll. From 1 to the weapon's Attack on Arms and armour. Bare hands do 1. Monsters do not roll: they deal their full Damage rating.
  2. Armour roll. Subtract a roll from 1 to the target's armour.
  3. Undead and immortal. Undead take half from weapons that are not magical. The immortal take nothing.
  4. Zero or less is a graze. The blow landed but did no harm.

Strength adds nothing to damage.

A fight, worked through

A party member with DEX 20, armour 8 and a Long Sword (Attack 15) meets the Orc (DEX 13, STR 15, Armour 2, Damage 12, HP 10).

  1. Your swing. The Orc's Dexterity this fight is 9 to 16, so your target is 9 to 13: 57% to 70% to hit, 64% on average.
  2. Your damage. 1–15, minus 1–2 for its armour. 10% of hits graze; the average hit does 6.5, and 30% of hits kill it outright.
  3. Its swing. The Orc bludgeons, so it uses STR 15 against your DEX 20. Its target is 17: 44% to hit you.
  4. Its damage. Always 12, minus 1–8 for your armour: 4 to 11, 7.5 on average.
  5. The kill. 10 ÷ 4, rounded down, + 1 = 3 XP, all to whoever lands the last blow.
  6. The loot. A chest 39% of the time; 36% of those chests are trapped.

What monsters drop

When a monster dies, the game rolls 1 to 30 against its Treasure rating. At or under the rating, it leaves a chest; otherwise a corpse. A second roll, under the rating this time, traps the chest. One number decides both, so the richer the monster, the likelier the trap.

Every creature's odds are in the Chest and Trapped columns of the Bestiary. Treasure 0, creatures that leave no body, and anything that dies on water or an archway never leave a chest. A trapped chest splashes acid on whoever opens it, for 1 to 30 damage, or poisons them: even odds.

Two creatures leave something else. A Gazer leaves a corpse and tries to put an Insect Swarm in its place. A Gargoyle crumbles into small rocks and leaves nothing.

Myths about combat

BUSTED EQUIP-0013

Strength makes your sword hit harder.

Damage is a roll from 1 to the weapon's rating, minus the target's armour roll. Strength is not part of it.

BUSTED EQUIP-0013

The Silver Sword slays the undead.

It counts as magical, so the undead take its full damage instead of half. The damage rules give it no extra power against them.

BUSTED COMBAT-0009 RNG-0001

The more treasure a monster carries, the safer its chest.

The opposite. The Treasure rating that makes a chest likely also makes a trap likely: a Dragon's chest is trapped 98% of the time.

Redux differs

A missed blow only lands on squares the weapon could reach. See Differences you'll notice.

Percentages assume fair dice, as the rules do when they quote odds. The 1988 original's random number source has not been recovered, so its real odds may differ a little.
